## What "Crossplay" Actually Means in Guns of Eschaton

"Crossplay" gets used as a catch-all term, but it usually bundles several separate features that games can turn on or off independently. A title can support crossplay matchmaking while offering no cross-progression at all — or vice versa. Understanding the difference tells you exactly what to expect before you buy a second copy for another platform.

| Feature | What it covers | What it does not cover |
|---|---|---|
| Crossplay matchmaking | Playing in the same lobbies and matches across platforms | Keeping your unlocks or rank |
| Cross-invite / party | Sending party invites and forming a squad across platforms | Random matchmaking pools |
| Cross-progression | Carrying level, unlocks, and stats between platforms | Playing together at the same time |
| Cross-save | Syncing a single save file across devices | Separate platform accounts |
| Cross-commerce | Buying DLC once and using it everywhere | Base game ownership |

For Guns of Eschaton, the practical question is almost always the first two rows: can you matchmake together, and can you party up together? Those two features often ship as a package, but community reports frequently show one working while the other is buggy or restricted.

### Crossplay vs. Cross-Input

A related but separate topic is input-based matchmaking. Some shooters separate controller and mouse-and-keyboard players into different pools by default, then let you opt into a mixed pool. If your crossplay is on but you still can't find a friend, input pool settings are the next thing to check.

| Setting | What it does | Who should change it |
|---|---|---|
| Crossplay toggle | Allows matching with other platforms | Everyone playing with friends off-platform |
| Input-based matchmaking | Separates controller and M&K lobbies | Players on mixed-input squads |
| Cross-invite / friend invites | Allows party invites across platforms | Anyone building a cross-platform squad |
| Cross-progression link | Ties accounts to one profile | Players who own the game twice |

### Why Console-to-Console Support Varies

Historically, console-to-console crossplay was the hardest pairing to negotiate, and many games still exclude it even when PC-to-console works fine. If you are planning a squad, build it around the pairing that is confirmed working today, not the one you hope gets added later.

## How to Enable (or Disable) Guns of Eschaton Crossplay

Turning crossplay on is usually a one-time setup, but it lives in different menus depending on platform. Work through the steps below in order, and confirm each one before moving to the next.

1. **Update the game first.** Crossplay features frequently require the current build on every platform in the party.
2. **Open the online or multiplayer settings menu.** Look for a "Crossplay," "Cross-Platform Play," or "Cross-Network Play" toggle.
3. **Set crossplay to On.** If you are on console, you may be prompted to accept an additional confirmation.
4. **Restart the game.** Many titles only apply network setting changes after a full restart, not a return to the main menu.
5. **Add friends through the in-game friends list**, not only the platform-level friends list.
6. **Host the party**, then send invites. The host's platform sometimes determines who can join.

| Step | Why it matters | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Updating the build | Mismatched versions cannot connect | Only the host updates |
| Enabling the toggle | Crossplay is often opt-in on console | Assuming it is on by default |
| Restarting the game | Settings apply on next launch | Backing out to the main menu |
| Using in-game friends list | Platform lists may not surface cross-platform players | Relying on the console overlay |
| Hosting the party | Some builds limit who can invite | Passing host to the wrong player |

If a friend still does not appear, have them send the invite instead. Invite direction matters more than most players expect, and community reports regularly show one direction working while the other silently fails.

## Guns of Eschaton Crossplay Troubleshooting

When crossplay is enabled but broken, the problem is usually version mismatch, privacy settings, or a platform-level restriction — not the game itself. Run through this checklist before you start reinstalling anything.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Friend is grayed out in the friends list | Privacy or "appear offline" setting | Set status to online and allow cross-platform visibility |
| Invites never arrive | Cross-invite not supported for that pairing | Try matchmaking into the same mode instead |
| "Cannot join session" error | Version mismatch | Both players update and fully restart |
| Matches connect but no voice | Voice chat is platform-locked | Use the game's party chat or a third-party app |
| Works one day, fails the next | Server-side or patch change | Check official channels for known issues |

If everything above checks out, the most likely explanation is that the pairing you are attempting simply is not supported yet. That is a design decision, not a bug, and no amount of router configuration will fix it.

### Quick Fixes That Solve Most Problems

- Power-cycle the console or restart the PC client completely.
- Switch from Wi-Fi to a wired connection on at least one side.
- Confirm both accounts are linked to the same platform profile they normally use.
- Disable VPNs and network-level ad blockers temporarily.
- Test with a third player to isolate whether the issue is account-specific.

## Party, Voice Chat, and Matchmaking Considerations

Crossplay changes more than who you can shoot at. It also affects how parties form, how voice chat routes, and how matchmaking balances a mixed squad. Player experience suggests that mixed-platform parties often face longer queue times, because the matchmaker has to find a lobby that accepts every platform in the group.

| Area | What usually works | What often does not |
|---|---|---|
| Party invites | In-game friends list | Platform overlay invites |
| Voice chat | Third-party apps | Native cross-platform voice |
| Matchmaking | Shared pool when crossplay is on | Guaranteed fast queues |
| Progression | Per-platform accounts | Automatic shared progress |

If you are planning a regular squad, agree on a voice solution before your first session. Native cross-platform voice is one of the least consistent features in crossplay games, and losing comms mid-match is far more disruptive than a slightly longer queue.

## Tips for Getting the Most Out of Cross-Platform Play

- **Pick one platform as the squad's home base** and host from there consistently.
- **Keep a shared chat channel** outside the game so you can coordinate when invites fail.
- **Update before every session.** A five-minute patch on one machine can block the whole party.
- **Check the mode.** Some game modes support crossplay while others are locked to a single platform pool.
- **Screenshot your settings** so you can restore them quickly after an update resets them.
- **Test with one friend first** before organizing a full squad, so you know which pairing works.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Guns of Eschaton crossplay available on all platforms?**
Support varies by pairing, and the only reliable answer is the one in your game's current build. Check the in-game online settings menu and the store listing for your platform, then test with a friend before planning a full squad around it.

**Does Guns of Eschaton crossplay include cross-progression?**
Not necessarily. Crossplay and cross-progression are separate features, and a game can support one without the other. If carrying your unlocks between platforms matters to you, verify progression support separately rather than assuming it comes with crossplay.

**Why can't my friend join my Guns of Eschaton crossplay lobby?**
The most common causes are a version mismatch, a private or offline privacy setting, or a pairing that only supports matchmaking rather than direct invites. Update, restart, check privacy settings, and try having your friend send the invite instead.

**Can I turn Guns of Eschaton crossplay off?**
Yes — most builds include a toggle in the online settings menu, and consoles often let you disable it at the system level too. Turning it off usually gives you faster queues within your own platform pool, at the cost of playing with friends elsewhere.

Crossplay is one of those features that is invisible when it works and maddening when it does not. Confirm your pairing, update every machine, set your privacy correctly, and agree on voice chat before the session starts. Do that, and the platform logo next to your friends' names stops mattering — which is exactly the point.
